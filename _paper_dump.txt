Abstract
We propose a ﬁve-tensor formalism for modelling intersubjective cognition in algorithmic-information-
theoretic terms. Building on the Memory–Code Asymmetry Principle (MCAP) and two earlier
scalar functionals — the existence density Ψ and the wisdom density ˆΨ — we adjoin a ﬁfth
tensor index representing a learnable family of perspective frames under which memory admits
distinct conditional Kolmogorov codings. The resulting scalar ˜Ψ S, the empathy density , is a
candidate quantitative measure of a system’s capacity to re-read its own memory under frames
of reference other than its own.
New in V ersion 7. The ﬁfth index is recast as a controllable information singularity : we
construct a blow-up operator Sm whose iterated application drives the perspective-conditioned
complexity to +∞ in a prescribed manner, and a retrieval operator Rµ that returns the system
to the ﬁnite bounded regime of human cognition (modelled by an entropy-budget µ). The main
control result (Theorem 13.7) shows that every trajectory admitting a bounded retrieval error
satisﬁes the ﬁnite-return inequality
0 ≤ ˜Ψ (post-retrieval)
S ≤ ˜Ψ (pre-singularity)
S + ε(µ),
where ε(µ) → 0 as the entropy budget tightens. The singularity is thus predictable in the sense
of Theorem 13.10, and controllable in the sense of Theorem 13.11.
T echnical contributions. We establish ten principal theorems, each with a complete proof,
supported by 22 lemmas, 9 propositions, and 7 worked computations. Fourteen ﬁgures (TikZ/pgf-
plots) and 23 tables of numerical data verify every inequality on a fully worked toy example
with D = J = L = M +1 = 2 and horizon T = 4, producing the triple
Ψ ≈ 6. 00, ˆΨ ≈ 7. 56, ˜Ψ ≈ 12. 4, WI ≈ 1. 26, EI ≈ 1. 64,
and exhibits two systems that Ψ , ˆΨ cannot distinguish but ˜Ψ separates by EI-gap ≈ 0. 10.
Keywords. Five-tensor, MCAP, perspective axis, empathy density, intersubjective cognition,
Kolmogorov complexity, information singularity, predictive control, theory of mind.

1 Introduction
1.1 Motivation
The Memory–Code Asymmetry Principle (MCAP) characterises intelligent systems as those whose mem-
ory dominates their code in Kolmogorov complexity and whose code is continually rewritten by memory.
Two earlier scalar functionals in this series evaluate a system from its own implicit standpoint:
Ψ S (existence), ˆΨ S (wisdom).
The present work introduces a third functional ˜Ψ S (empathy) whose underlying mathematical object is
a ﬁve-index tensor. The ﬁfth index, the perspective axis m, represents a learnable family of reference
frames under which memory admits distinct Kolmogorov codings.
Version 7 of this monograph adds a control-theoretic interpretation of the perspective axis: m is a
controllable information singularity . Roughly:
• Iterating a blow-up operator Sm drives perspective- conditioned complexity K(M | E, Pm) to +∞
at a prescribed rate (Theorem 13.2).
• A retrieval operator Rµ returns the system to a bounded regime (“human cognition”), with bounded
loss (Theorem 13.7).
• The trajectory is predictable: a ﬁnite-parameter predictor PΘ tracks ˜Ψ along blow-ups with error
O(1/T ) (Theorem 13.10).
This conforms with the philosophical demand that genuinely intersubjective cognition should be able to
contemplate “the unbounded” while retaining the capacity to return to a bounded interlocutor.
1.2 Principal claims
We establish ten principal theorems:
1. Boundedness (Theorem 9.1).
2. MCAP equivalence (Theorem 9.3).
3. Empathy threshold existence (Theorem 9.5).
4. Explicit upper bound on β∗ (Theorem 9.10).
5. Solipsism collapse (Theorem 10.1).
6. Perspective monotonicity (Theorem 10.3).
7. Overwrite monotonicity (Theorem 10.4).
8. Compassion decomposition (Theorem 10.6).
9. Trichotomy independence (Theorem 11.1).
10. Cauchy convergence at rate O(ϵ/T ) (Theorem 11.3).
Three additional theorems govern the singularity structure: S-blow-up (Theorem 13.2), predictability
(Theorem 13.10), controllability with ﬁnite retrieval (Theorem 13.7).
